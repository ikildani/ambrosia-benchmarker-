# IP Map: Customer Segments, Market Size and Willingness to Pay

Research date: 2026-09-28. About 20 search and fetch calls. Every figure has a label: **[Published]** means a primary or vendor source states it. **[Reported]** means a secondary, aggregator or blog source reports it, so treat it as directional. **[Estimate]** means my own inference. Many vendors (Clarivate, PatSnap, LexisNexis PatentSight, Questel Orbit, Wellspring, IN-PART) do not publish prices. The Gaps sections list those unknowns openly and do not fill them in.

## 1. University and research-institute technology transfer offices (TTOs)

### Takeaway
The US and Canadian TTO market is small and countable: about 338 AUTM member institutions were invited to the FY2024 survey and 204 responded. These offices do about 8,800 licenses or options a year, and gross licensing income is about $3.6–3.8B. Budgets are under pressure: licensing income fell 24% and licensing staff fell more than 4% in FY2024. Their current software spend goes to IP and portfolio management systems (Inteum, Wellspring) that cost from a few thousand dollars to low six figures a year. Their marketing spend goes to matchmaking tools (IN-PART). IP Map would have to sit in the "marketing and licensee identification" budget line, which is small per office.

### Cited Findings
- **[Published]** AUTM FY2024 US survey: research funding $109B. Licensing staff declined more than 4%. Gross licensing income fell 24%. Running royalties dropped 41%. Startups rose 5%. New products rose 8%. Canada: 31 institutions, research funding CAD $8B, licensing staff grew 3.5%, gross licensing income rose 13.5% — [AUTM FY2024 Licensing Survey](https://autm.net/surveys-and-tools/surveys/licensing-survey/2024licensingsurvey)
- **[Published]** AUTM FY2024 response base: 204 responses out of 338 invited member institutions (60.4%). AUTM reporters are "between a quarter and a third of the institutions that report to HERD" — [NSF NCSES, Science & Engineering Indicators 2026, SATRN-2](https://ncses.nsf.gov/pubs/nsb20262/table/SATRN-2)
- **[Published]** AUTM FY2023 US: research expenditures $104B, license income $3.6B, 714 new commercial products, 25,000+ invention disclosures. Patent licenses were about 3,000, copyright licenses 3,200+ and other license types 1,600+. Startups fell 11% — [AUTM FY2023 Licensing Survey](https://autm.net/surveys-and-tools/surveys/licensing-survey/2023-licensing-survey)
- **[Published]** US universities issued almost 8,800 new licenses or options in 2021. Startups and small businesses took 78% of new licenses or options in 2021, up from 69% in 2011 — [NSF NCSES Indicators, Fig INV-13](https://ncses.nsf.gov/pubs/nsb20241/figure/INV-13)
- **[Reported]** US and Canadian universities, hospitals and research institutes reported about $3.8B gross licensing income in a recent AUTM survey, which also noted a standing population of about 7,000 university startups — [LincolnLabs summary of AUTM](https://lincolnlabs.com/university-tech-transfer-by-the-numbers-licenses-royalties-startups/)
- **[Published, procurement record]** NIH/HHS enterprise IP management system (patenting and licensing of government inventions), fixed-price purchase order with a base year plus four 12-month options. Inteum's total evaluated price was **$1,136,377**. Wellspring's was **$6,245,894**, about 5.5x Inteum's and about 290% above the revised government estimate. The award went to Inteum in 2019 — [GAO B-417282.2 et al., Wellspring Worldwide](https://www.gao.gov/products/b-417282.2,b-417282.3,b-417282.4). Roughly $227K a year for Inteum and $1.25M a year for Wellspring. This is the largest possible enterprise case (all of HHS), not a typical university office.
- **[Reported]** Inteum's list pricing "starts at US$200/month". Wellspring does not publish pricing — [Capterra Canada, Sophia vs Inteum](https://www.capterra.ca/compare/130264/163831/sophia/vs/inteum)
- **[Published, vendor]** Wellspring says 200+ universities and research organizations use Wellspring for Tech Transfer — [Wellspring Tech Transfer](https://www.wellspring.com/technology-transfer)

### Inferences
- **[Estimate]** Addressable US and Canadian buyers are about 300–350 active offices, rising to about 400–500 if non-AUTM-reporting hospitals and institutes are counted. A global total including Europe (ASTP), UK (PraxisAuril), Asia and Australia is plausibly 1,000–1,500 offices. I did not verify a global count.
- **[Estimate]** Life sciences probably account for most licensing income, though I found no current AUTM split (see Gaps). About 8,800 licenses a year across the whole population works out to roughly 25–30 per office per year on average, and the distribution is heavily skewed toward the top 30–50 offices.
- **[Estimate]** Falling licensing income in FY2024 plus staff cuts point to price sensitivity. Pricing that looks realistic for a TTO is about $5K–$25K a year per office for a licensee-ranking tool, or per-case pricing for a single technology. The top 30–50 offices could support $25K–$50K. This is anchored to the fact that system-of-record software, the office's core system, costs in the low tens of thousands at mid-size offices.
- The NIH award shows public-sector buyers select on price and will pass over the premium vendor.

### Gaps
- I could not reach the FY2024 absolute counts of licenses, options and staff FTEs. The AUTM page gives only percentage changes, and the full report sits behind AUTM STATT or membership.
- I found no current published life-science share of licenses or income. Historically it is cited as the majority, but I could not confirm this.
- IN-PART, Wellspring university, Knowledge Sharing and Ambercite prices were not found. No public university purchase order surfaced in search. A targeted search of state university board minutes or open-records portals (for example Texas and California purchasing databases) would be the next step.
- I found no source on how many TTOs subscribe to PatSnap, Derwent or Orbit.

## 2. Biotech asset sellers (venture-backed and public biotechs seeking partners)

### Takeaway
There are a few thousand US biotechs, and this is the largest count segment. Today they run partnering through BIO and other partnering meetings, through BD consultants on monthly retainers, and through bankers on success fees. Advisor retainers of $25K–$75K a month make even a $10K–$30K data or report product look cheap.

### Cited Findings
- **[Reported]** IBISWorld counts 3,322 biotechnology businesses in the US as of 2026, up 4.5% from 2025 and growing 4.8% a year since 2021. This is an industry-code count and includes non-therapeutics companies — [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/industry-statistics/number-of-businesses/biotechnology-united-states/)
- **[Published]** BIO 2025 (Boston): 20,000+ attendees, 10,000+ partnering delegates, more than 62,000 partnering meetings scheduled — [BIO 2025 facts and figures](https://convention.bio.org/about-bio/bio-2025-numbers); [BioSpectrum Asia](https://www.biospectrumasia.com/news/89/26201/bio-international-convention-2025-opens-in-boston-bringing-together-20000-global-biotech-leaders.html)
- **[Reported]** Boutique BD advisors doing partner searches usually charge monthly retainers of $25K–$75K plus success fees of 1–5% of deal value — [Vision Life Sciences guide](https://visionlifesciences.com/insights/top-biotech-consulting-firms)
- **[Reported]** Finder's fees in PE and M&A usually run 2–5% of capital raised, or 1–3% for larger M&A — [ReferralHero](https://referralhero.com/blog/finders-fees)
- **[Reported]** Pharma buyers are "flooded with incoming requests, reviewing several hundred opportunities annually". This shows the seller-side problem is targeting, not volume — [PatSnap Eureka blog](https://eureka.patsnap.com/blog/life-science/pharma-licensing-deal-evaluation/)

### Inferences
- **[Estimate]** The number of therapeutics biotechs actively seeking a partner at any moment is plausibly 1,000–2,000 in the US. That estimate uses the BIO delegate count (10K delegates, of whom perhaps 3–5K are companies) and the IBISWorld base. About 5,000+ worldwide is likely, including China, Korea, Europe and Israel, where Solidus already converts well.
- **[Estimate]** One partner-search engagement costs $75K–$225K for a 3-month retainer, before any success fee. A per-asset "licensee ranking report" priced at $2.5K–$10K, like the Solidus Brief at $2,500, or a $10K–$25K yearly seat, is a small fraction of that and an easy buy for a CEO or CBO.
- Note that a success fee can create a broker-dealer or ethics problem (see memory: Ambrosia is retainer-only). IP Map should not be priced on success.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative count of venture-backed versus public therapeutics biotechs for 2025–26. BIO, EY Beyond Borders and Evaluate publish counts behind paywalls.
- LSX, BIO-Europe and JPM partnering attendance and fees were not retrieved.
- Banker fees on licensing deals, as opposed to M&A, were not found in a primary source.

## 3. Pharma BD, search and evaluation (S&E) and licensing teams

### Takeaway
There are relatively few pharma buyers, but they have the most budget per organization. They already pay $10K–$100K+ a year for deal, pipeline and forecast data (DealForma, Evaluate, Cortellis), plus outsourced S&E consulting. For them, IP Map fits as a module next to, or bundled into, a deal-intelligence seat, not as a new category.

### Cited Findings
- **[Published, vendor]** DealForma: Individual plan $18,000 a year (1 user, 10 analyst hours). Premium $32,000 a year (2 users, 20 analyst hours, Tableau dashboards, quarterly trend reports). Group or Enterprise (3+ users, API) is custom priced — [DealForma pricing](https://dealforma.com/pricing/)
- **[Reported]** Evaluate Pharma entry pricing is about $10K–$20K per seat per year. Enterprise deals with expert interviews run $50K–$100K+ a year. Cortellis does not publish prices and is sold per seat and per module on annual terms through procurement — [Salesmotion, Clarivate alternatives](https://salesmotion.io/clarivate-alternatives); [IntuitionLabs Cortellis guide](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/clarivate-cortellis-guide)
- **[Published]** Universities hold institutional Cortellis licenses. For example, HBS Baker Library has Cortellis Competitive Intelligence Executive Base and Clinical Trials Intelligence Professional — [HBS Baker Library](https://www.library.hbs.edu/databases-cases-and-more/databases/cortellis)
- **[Published, vendor]** Outsourced S&E and asset scouting is a service line at Alacrita and Evaluate Consulting. In one Alacrita engagement, 5,500 potential assets were screened, leading to contact with 51 distressed companies about 99 assets — [Alacrita case study](https://www.alacrita.com/case-studies/search-for-licensable-pharma-assets-of-financially-distressed-companies); [Evaluate BD&L consulting](https://www.evaluate.com/consulting/business-development-and-licensing-consulting/)

### Inferences
- **[Estimate]** Buyers number roughly 20 big pharmas, 50–100 mid-caps and specialty pharmas, and a growing group of Japanese, Korean and Chinese pharmas that in-license (Daewoong and Fosun are already in the Ambrosia network). That makes about 150–300 organizations worldwide with active in-licensing. Big-pharma S&E teams are commonly described as 10–50+ people, but I found no source for that (see Gaps).
- **[Estimate]** Buyers want IP Map's exclusivity forecast and claim-strength score during diligence. The "likely licensee" ranking matters less to them, although a buyer could use it to see where competing bidders sit. Realistic willingness to pay is $15K–$50K a year per team as an add-on, anchored to DealForma at $18–32K and Evaluate at $10–20K per seat.

### Gaps
- I found no published S&E team sizes or number of in-licensing companies. Cortellis, GlobalData, Citeline and PharmaVentures prices were not found in a primary source.

## 4. Life-science investors (VC and crossover) doing IP diligence

### Takeaway
Investors buy IP diligence mostly as law-firm work, through FTO and patentability opinions and diligence memos. Reported prices run about $10K–$50K+ per opinion. A tool that pre-screens claim strength and exclusivity would replace or narrow part of that spend, not remove the legal opinion.

### Cited Findings
- **[Reported]** FTO opinions usually range from $10K to $50K+, depending on complexity and jurisdictions. A basic landscape assessment is $3K–$5K. A comprehensive multi-jurisdiction analysis can exceed $50K — [LeanLaw](https://www.leanlaw.co/blog/how-to-structure-freedom-to-operate-fto-opinions-capping-costs-while-defining-clear-search-scope/)
- **[Reported]** Another source puts FTO opinions at $10K–$30K and up — [UpCounsel](https://www.upcounsel.com/freedom-to-operate)
- **[Published]** The AIPLA 2025 Report of the Economic Survey (released February 2026) contains hourly rates and typical charges for prosecution and opinion tasks, but it is members-only or sold separately — [AIPLA 2025 Economic Survey](https://www.aipla.org/home/news-publications/economic-survey/2025-report-of-the-economic-survey)
- **[Reported]** US biotech venture capital raised was $20.6B in 2025 — [EY Beyond Borders 2026 via search summary](https://www.ey.com/en_us/life-sciences/biotech-outlook)

### Inferences
- **[Estimate]** Active life-science VC and crossover firms number about 300–500 in the US and about 1,000 worldwide. Each firm does a handful to a few dozen diligences a year. At $10K–$50K of outside IP counsel per serious deal, a $20K–$50K yearly tool pays for itself if it screens out a few opinions or shortens their scope.
- Investor diligence is the natural bridge to Augur, which is on hold per memory.

### Gaps
- The exact AIPLA FTO and validity opinion medians are paywalled. Buying the report (about a few hundred dollars for non-members) would give a primary anchor.
- I found no data on how much VCs spend on IP tools as opposed to counsel.

## 5. IP law firms and patent monetization or licensing firms

### Takeaway
Law firms and monetization firms already pay $15K–$80K+ a year for patent analytics platforms. Brokers earn success fees of 15–40% of proceeds. This is strong evidence that monetizing patent rights, including finding the buyer, commands a large share of deal value.

### Cited Findings
- **[Reported]** PatSnap entry contracts are $15K–$30K a year for small seat counts. Adding modules pushes contracts toward $50K+. Enterprise deployments reach "well into six figures". Competing quotes are worth 20–30% in negotiation (buyer reports as of July 2026) — [Beyond Elevation, PatSnap pricing 2026](https://beyondelevation.com/blog/posts/patsnap-pricing-2026/)
- **[Reported]** PatSnap, Derwent Innovation and Orbit Intelligence each cost about $25K–$80K a year. Derwent starts at about £20K a year and is reported at "five figures per seat per year" — [Beyond Elevation, Derwent alternatives](https://beyondelevation.com/blog/posts/derwent-innovation-alternatives-2026/)
- **[Reported]** Patent broker success fees usually run 25–35% of gross proceeds (range 15–40%). Some brokers charge upfront fees of $5K–$15K for evaluation and marketing materials — [UpCounsel, patent broker fees](https://www.upcounsel.com/patent-broker-fees); [LARUS](https://larus.net/blog/understanding-ip-brokerage-fees-contracts/)
- **[Reported]** One tiered broker model charges 7.5% seller + 2.5% buyer under $50K, falling to 4% + 1% above $1M — [Ipiry](https://www.ipiry.com/patent-brokerage)

### Inferences
- These broker numbers come mostly from tech and ICT patent sales, not biopharma licenses, where the partner is an operating pharma and the deal is milestone-heavy. They still show high willingness to pay for finding the buyer.

### Gaps
- I found no public LexisNexis PatentSight, Questel Orbit or Cipher pricing. I found no fee survey for law-firm analytics budgets.

## 6. Pricing benchmarks and willingness to pay for licensee identification

### Takeaway
Comparable tools range from about $3K a year (DrugPatentWatch Basic) to $18–32K (DealForma) to $25–80K+ (PatSnap, Derwent, Orbit), with enterprise contracts in six figures. Service alternatives cost much more: FTO opinions at $10–50K, partner-search retainers at $25–75K a month, and broker fees of 15–40%. That supports a laddered price: per-asset reports, a professional seat and team or enterprise tiers, plus a bundle discount inside Solidus.

### Inferences (pricing recommendation inputs, all [Estimate])
- **Per-asset licensee ranking report: $2,500–$7,500.** This sits above a landscape assessment ($3–5K) and far below an advisor retainer month ($25–75K). It matches the existing $2,500 Brief and serves sellers and TTOs.
- **Standalone professional seat: about $12K–$20K a year.** This is above DrugPatentWatch Ultimate ($8K) and below DealForma Individual ($18K) and PatSnap entry ($15–30K). It fits biotech BD, VCs and the top TTOs.
- **Team or enterprise: about $30K–$75K a year.** This matches PatSnap, Derwent and Orbit bands and targets pharma S&E and law or monetization firms.
- **Tiered TTO pricing by office size, about $5K–$25K**, is needed because TTO budgets are shrinking (FY2024 income down 24%).
- **Bundle into Solidus Pro or Enterprise** at about 25–40% below the combined standalone prices. Evaluate and DealForma bundle analyst hours and reports in the same way.
- **Sizing sketch [Estimate]:** TTOs about 350 US at about $10K ≈ $3.5M. Biotech sellers about 1,500 US at about $5K average (report or seat mix) ≈ $7.5M. Pharma about 200 worldwide at about $40K ≈ $8M. VCs about 400 at about $15K ≈ $6M. Law and monetization firms are a few hundred at about $20K. The US-led serviceable market is roughly $25–35M ARR, and about 2x that worldwide. These are rough order-of-magnitude figures.

### Gaps
- I found no published price for a commissioned "licensee search" study as a separate deliverable. Consultancies (Alacrita, Evaluate Consulting, PharmaVentures) do not post fees. The advisor retainer range above is the closest proxy.
- I found no primary price for PatentSight, Orbit, IN-PART, Knowledge Sharing or Ambercite. A useful next step is a FOIA or open-records request, or searching state procurement portals for "IN-PART" and "PatSnap" university purchase orders.
- I found no biopharma-specific data on patent-brokerage fees.
