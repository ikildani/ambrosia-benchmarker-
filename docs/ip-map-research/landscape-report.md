# IP Map should rank buyers, not technology

The patent analytics market is crowded and well funded, but it is not organized around the question IP Map is built to answer: **who, specifically, will pay for this patent, and how strong is it?** The general suites (LexisNexis PatentSight+, Clarivate Derwent, Questel Orbit, PatSnap, Anaqua AcclaimIP) score portfolio value and draw citation maps. The pharma tools (DrugPatentWatch, Pharsight, Cortellis, Citeline Biomedtracker, Patsnap Synapse) map drugs to patents and loss-of-exclusivity (LOE) dates. Only two vendors, Ambercite and PatSnap's 2017 "Licensee Locator," explicitly market citation-driven licensee finding, and neither publishes its method or any validation. No platform in the research normalizes citing activity by the citing company's own filing volume. None scores indefinite claim language. None was found linking patent positions to licensing or M&A deal terms. Those three gaps line up exactly with IP Map's two core metrics and with what Solidus already has: a verified deal database, an outcome ledger and an entity graph of about 28,000 companies and 55,000 assets. The academic evidence supports both metrics, with two corrections. Citations predict *whether* a patent is licensed or acquired far better than *how much* it is worth. And a raw count of "about"/"around" is a weak proxy for legal risk, because courts treat terms of degree as indefinite only when the specification gives no objective standard. IP Map's defensible position is a **deal-backtested, filer-normalized buyer ranking, shown as a map, with every rank explained**. Incumbents can copy the visuals, but not the deal ledger underneath them.

## Incumbents sell maps of technology, not maps of buyers

The general-purpose market has consolidated into three houses plus specialists. **LexisNexis (RELX) now owns PatentSight, Cipher and IPlytics**, and has built Cipher's classifiers into "PatentSight+" ([LexisNexis Cipher deal](https://www.lexisnexisip.com/resources/lexisnexis-enters-into-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-aistemos-and-its-cipher-classification-platform/); [IPlytics deal](https://www.lexisnexisip.com/resources/lexisnexis-expands-its-intellectual-property-solutions-suite-with-the-acquisition-of-iplytics/)). Clarivate has renamed Innography "Derwent Patent Analytics" ([Clarivate](https://clarivate.com/intellectual-property/derwent/patent-analytics/)), and AcclaimIP now sits inside Anaqua ([Anaqua](https://www.anaqua.com/resources/patent-scores-and-how-to-use-them-acclaimip/)). Their flagship scores all measure value or importance, not buyer fit. PatentSight's **Competitive Impact = Technology Relevance × Market Coverage**, with citations corrected for age, patent office and technology field and scaled so the average is 1.0 ([LexisNexis PAI whitepaper](https://www.lexisnexisip.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/The-Patent-Asset-Index.pdf)). Orbit's Technology Impact is the same idea, and its Patent Value adds country GDP and remaining patent life ([Orbit value metrics](https://intelligence.help.questel.com/en/support/solutions/articles/77000473131-patent-metrics-value-metrics)). AcclaimIP's P-Score is a percentile composite of citation, technology-class and legal sub-scores ([Anaqua](https://www.anaqua.com/resources/patent-scores-and-how-to-use-them-acclaimip/)). Innography's PatentStrength combines "over 30 predictive variables" trained on how likely a patent is to be litigated ([Innography](https://www.innography.com/why-innography/patentstrength)). All of them are cross-industry. None is tuned to biotech realities such as regulatory exclusivity or Orange Book listing.

Licensee finding is the closest thing to IP Map's first metric, and it is thinly served. **PatSnap announced Licensee Locator in December 2017.** It takes a patent or text, "rank[s] a shortlist of potential licensees" and "gauge[s] companies' likelihood to license, based on previous deals" ([Research Live](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/patsnap-launches-automated-tool-for-rd-licensing/id/5031572)). But PatSnap's current pricing page lists no such module, so the feature may have been folded into another product or dropped ([PatSnap pricing](https://www.patsnap.com/pricing)). **Ambercite** runs a "Licensing Deal Potential Index" on citation-network similarity. It aggregates results by company and surfaces "hidden links" through indirect citation paths ([Ambercite blog](https://www.ambercite.com/amberblog/2024/8/29/ambercite-your-treasure-map-to-quickly-find-companies-for-licensing-your-technology-in-a-180-billion-market)). The index's formula is unpublished. The main article advocating the method was written by someone associated with Ambercite, and its validation is two litigated examples ([LESI](https://lesi.org/article-of-the-month/advanced-citation-analysis-can-help-identify-licensing-candidates/)). Everyone else (Orbit, Derwent, PatSnap's citation map, Patent iNSIGHT Pro, See-the-Forest) hands the user a list or map of citing assignees and leaves the ranking to them ([Questel citation search](https://www.questel.com/communication/citations-search-en.html); [Patent iNSIGHT Pro](https://www.patentinsightpro.com/); [See-the-Forest](http://www.see-the-forest.com/help/general/maps_interconnection.php)).

The pharma-specific tier has a different problem: a commoditized core. Drug-to-patent mapping with LOE dates is offered by DrugPatentWatch, Pharsight, Cortellis Generics Intelligence, Biomedtracker and Patsnap Synapse ([DPW](https://www.drugpatentwatch.com/subscribe.php); [Pharsight](https://pharsight.greyb.com/); [Clinical Leader on Citeline](https://www.clinicalleader.com/doc/citeline-s-pharmaprojects-biomedtracker-solution-0001); [Patsnap Synapse](https://synapse.patsnap.com/blog/exploring-the-world-of-sequence-ip-with-patsnap-bio)). All of them rest mostly on public Orange Book, exclusivity and litigation data, and cheap repackagers are already selling LOE calendars on Apify ([Apify](https://apify.com/priori_intelligence/priori-drug-loss-of-exclusivity-calendar)). Only the large suites hold patents and deals under one roof. **Cortellis Deals Intelligence claims more than 90,000 licensing and M&A deals** ([IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/clarivate-cortellis-guide)), and Biomedtracker covers patent expiry and deal activity in the same product. In their public material, though, the two sit in separate modules, and none of it shows remaining exclusivity or patent strength driving deal economics or buyer ranking. The well-funded AI-native wave is aimed elsewhere. **Patlytics ($40M Series B, $65M total) and Solve Intelligence ($40M Series B)** sell drafting, prosecution and infringement workflows to law firms, not biotech asset intelligence ([TAMradar](https://www.tamradar.com/funding-rounds/patlytics-series-b-40m); [Patentext](https://patentext.com/blog/solve-intelligence-vs-patlytics)).

## Four gaps no incumbent closes

The first gap is **normalization by filer volume**. Derwent, Google Patents and AcclaimIP expose whether a citation came from the examiner or the applicant ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7433884B2/en); [Anaqua](https://www.anaqua.com/resources/patent-scores-and-how-to-use-them-acclaimip/)). PatentSight and Orbit normalize citations for age and field. But no platform found asks whether a company cites the target *more than its patenting volume would predict*. Without that correction, a raw ranking of citing assignees in biotech mostly reproduces the list of the largest filers. For IP Map's first metric that is the difference between a useful signal and a list of big pharma companies.

The second gap is **the link from patents to deals**. The only transaction signals in the general suites are PatSnap's 2017 "previous deals" likelihood and AcclaimIP's class-level assignment rates. No vendor was found tying citations to biopharma licensing or M&A terms. No public product description links remaining exclusivity to upfront or royalty comps, or ranks buyers by their own LOE exposure in a therapeutic area. This is the whitespace Solidus is uniquely placed to fill. It needs one caveat: from outside, the research could not confirm whether Cortellis or Biomedtracker join a deal record to the asset's patent expiry inside their user interface.

The third gap is **claim-level analytics, especially biotech claim types**. AcclaimIP folds independent-claim length into its legal score, and Innography buries claim variables among its 30-plus predictors. **No platform found counts claim elements or scores indefinite terms such as "about" or "substantially."** In biologics the market splits in two. Sequence tools (Lens PatSeq, with 500M+ sequences; GenomeQuest; Patsnap Bio) say which patents read on a sequence or antibody CDR ([Lens PatSeq](https://about.lens.org/patseq-exploring-biological-sequences-in-patents/); [Aptean GenomeQuest datasheet](https://assets.ctfassets.net/grb5fvwhwnyo/59wBBUyObjxJD7wqTgqmwo/16e558cd560fcd3c383903bb4e1ebe92/Aptean-GenomeQuest-Antibody-Module-Datasheet-en.pdf)). Drug tools say when protection ends. Nothing ties the two together per asset. The Purple Book makes this worse: it lists only patents disclosed in the BPCIA "patent dance," so its patent lists are incomplete by design ([Center for Biosimilars](https://www.centerforbiosimilars.com/view/opinion-purple-book-patent-listings-are-only-a-first-step)).

The fourth gap is **showing the work**. No vendor in either tier publishes back-tested accuracy for its licensee indices, value scores or LOE dates. Cortellis reduces LOE to one date per country without exposing which patent, exclusivity or SPC sets it ([Clarivate](https://clarivate.com/life-sciences-healthcare/manufacturing-supply-chain-intelligence/product-intelligence-analytics/)). User and commentator feedback on the visual suites says the same thing: the maps impress but "demand manual configuration before they produce insight" (a low-reliability source, though consistent across tools) ([beyondelevation](https://beyondelevation.com/blog/posts/patent-data-visualization-compared/)). None of the maps reviewed shows deal status or licensee fit on top of the citation network. IP Map's outcome ledger turns this gap into a product feature: every buyer ranking becomes a logged prediction that is later scored against real deals.

## The evidence backs both metrics, with two corrections

### Citation frequency predicts whether a deal happens, not what it is worth

Forward citations are a real but noisy value signal, and the effect has replicated for decades. **Patents renewed to full term are cited significantly more than lapsed ones** ([Harhoff et al. 1999](https://direct.mit.edu/rest/article/81/3/511/57132/Citation-Frequency-and-the-Value-of-Patented)). **One extra citation per patent raises firm market value by about 3%** in Tobin's q regressions, and self-citations carry *more* value than external ones ([Hall, Jaffe & Trajtenberg, NBER w7741](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7741)). **Citations added by examiners predict renewal better than citations added by applicants** ([Hegde & Sampat 2009](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1807078)). Litigated patents are cited much more often than their cohort peers ([Lanjouw & Schankerman, NBER w6297](https://www.nber.org/papers/w6297)). For IP Map's purpose, the most important finding is the most sobering one. At major US research universities, **citations predict whether a technology gets licensed but not the revenue it earns** ([Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/1-4020-2755-9_13); effect size not extracted). The product should therefore present citation-derived scores as *propensity*, with valuation left to Solidus's deal comps.

Citation data has also degraded. **A small minority of applications now generates most citations, and citing and cited patents have become less technologically similar.** Weighting citations by text similarity substantially restores their predictive power ([Kuhn, Younge & Marco 2020](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2714954)). The literature points toward directional, company-to-company signals. Citation activity between an acquirer and its target is an important driver of acquisition premiums ([Long Range Planning 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0024630125000378); full text inaccessible, no effect size). Patent-portfolio similarity forecasts M&A pairs, and an interpretable similarity algorithm (MASS) beats a LightGCN graph network in most settings ([Albora et al., arXiv 2404.07179](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.07179)). Citations used in Office Action *rejections* are being studied as a separate, higher-signal layer ([Cotropia 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jels.12404)). There is one trap for backtesting. **After an exclusive university license, forward citations from non-licensees increase** ([J. Econ. Behavior & Org.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167268117300847)), so citations can follow deals as well as precede them. Any backtest has to measure citation windows strictly *before* the deal date, or it will leak the answer.

Taken together, this defines "relative citation frequency" more precisely than the current brief does. For each candidate company *F* and each monetized patent family *P*, compute a **Citation Lift**: *F*'s share of forward citations to *P*, divided by *F*'s expected share given its filing volume in the same CPC group and cohort. The inputs should be counted family-to-family (as LexisNexis does, per [LexisNexis](https://support.lexisnexisip.com/hc/en-us/articles/20133304698259-What-is-Family-to-Family-Citation)), with examiner citations weighted up, weights for text similarity, and weights for the citing company's own citation-dumping rate. The owner's self-citations are not buyers, but they should be kept as a signal of how much the owner itself relies on the family, not thrown away. For multi-generation paths, the 2004 CHI Research patent US7433884B2 already describes summed citation-path weights of 1.0 / 0.5 / 0.333 across generations ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7433884B2/en)). Its priority date suggests the term has run, but confirm its status before copying the method. No peer-reviewed study tests citations for predicting *pharma in-licensing counterparties* specifically. That is both the risk and the opportunity: IP Map would be first to publish such a backtest, and it would do so on Solidus's own deal data.

### Claim breadth is well measured; "about" counts need legal tiers

The breadth half of the second metric rests on solid ground. **Word count of the shortest independent claim** (shorter means broader) and **independent-claim count** are validated scope proxies that can be computed before and after examination ([Marco, Sarnoff & deGrazia 2019](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/respol/v48y2019i926.html)). Kuhn and Thompson show that the traditional proxies (number of patent classes, forward citations and total claim count) are uninformative or misleading measures of scope. They also show that narrowing a patent's scope makes it much less likely to be declared standard-essential ([Kuhn & Thompson](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2977273)). Two design rules follow. First, normalize word counts by CPC group and year. Second, show **breadth and importance as separate axes**, never blended into one score. The USPTO's Patent Claims Research Dataset already publishes claim-level word counts and dependency structure for granted patents *and* published applications ([USPTO PCRD](https://www.uspto.gov/ip-policy/economic-research/research-datasets/patent-claims-research-dataset)). So "prosecution narrowing" (application claims compared with granted claims) comes almost free, and it is a strong signal of estoppel and examiner toughness.

The indefiniteness half needs redesigning. Under *Nautilus*, a claim is indefinite only if it fails to inform a skilled artisan of its scope "with reasonable certainty" in light of the specification and prosecution history ([Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2014/06/supreme-court-decides-nautilus-v-biosig)). **Terms of degree are not inherently indefinite.** "Unobtrusive manner" failed because it was purely subjective ([Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/post-nautilus-look-at-terms-of-degree-and-measuring-terms.html)). In November 2025, "optimal" and "best" failed because the specification lacked objective boundaries ([Morgan Lewis on Akamai v. MediaPointe](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/12/federal-circuit-reinforces-indefiniteness-standard-for-terms-of-degree)). A raw frequency of "about" and "around" will therefore generate false alarms. This matters especially in pharma, where "about" usually qualifies a numeric dose or range. The research found **no quantified validity-failure rate for claims containing "about" or "substantially."** The better build is a tiered flag. Subjective or evaluative terms are high risk. Degree terms with no numeric anchor or definition in the specification are medium risk. Defined terms are low risk. A separate flag should cover a claimed parameter whose measurement method is unspecified, which is a recognized indefiniteness route directly relevant to formulation and PK claims ([Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/post-nautilus-look-at-terms-of-degree-and-measuring-terms.html)). The raw counts should still be shown, because users will ask for them, but they should not drive the risk rating.

The biggest open question is biotech-specific. For antibody and small-molecule claims, genus and Markush breadth under *Amgen v. Sanofi* enablement, and written-description risk, arguably matter more than indefiniteness. The research did not cover them. CAS MARPAT (1.16M+ Markush structures) and Derwent DWPIM (1.9M) are the only structured sources for chemical genus claims, and both are expert tools with non-public pricing ([CAS MARPAT](https://www.cas.org/training/documentation/markush); [Clarivate CPI](https://clarivate.com/intellectual-property/derwent/chemical-patents-index/)). No tool was found that makes functional antibody claims structurally searchable. Tagging each independent claim as sequence-defined, functional genus, Markush, method-of-use or formulation would be new. It should be confirmed with patent practitioners before it is built, and it needs a dedicated legal research pass.

## Ranked feature set: build on what Solidus already owns

The ranking below weighs three things: strength of evidence, how hard the feature is for an incumbent to copy, and the cost of the data. The first three items are the product. The rest turn it from a score into a workflow.

The recommended order is to start with interpretable features and add learned models later. MASS beating LightGCN in most settings ([arXiv 2404.07179](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.07179)) suggests a transparent similarity model plus a deal prior is also the stronger model, not merely the easier one to explain. Every score should come with its three to five top contributing factors.

## A visual-first interface built around decisions

The research gives a clear rule for when a patent map is useful. **A map works when node position means something, edges carry direction and type, and a click leads to a decision.** It fails when it is an all-patent hairball ([WIPO open-source manual](https://wipo-analytics.github.io/manual/gephi-1.html); [ForceAtlas2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4051631/)). Every view below therefore has a ranked, exportable list beside it, a "why is this ranked here" explanation on each node, a time slider and a filter for citation type. ForceAtlas2 is designed for roughly 10 to 10,000 nodes, and aggregating citations to companies rather than patents keeps the main views within that range.

| View | What it shows | Decision it serves |
|---|---|---|
| **Constellation** (hero view) | The monetized family at the center. Companies orbit it, with distance = inverse Citation Lift, size = deal capacity or BD activity from Solidus, ring color = prior deals in the therapeutic area, and edge style = examiner vs applicant. Click a company to open the specific citing patents | Who to call first and why |
| **Buyer Board** | The ranked list paired with Constellation. For each company it shows propensity, contributing factors, comparable Solidus deals and LOE exposure in the therapeutic area | Outreach shortlist and pitch angle |
| **Citation Flow** | A Sankey diagram of company-to-company citation flows across time windows | Whether interest is rising, and who has moved in recently |
| **Claim Anatomy** | The dependency tree of the claims. The independent claim is annotated with word count against the CPC benchmark, elements, tiered indefiniteness highlights and an application-vs-grant redline | How strong the asset is and what a buyer's counsel will attack |
| **Claim Strength Grid** | Small multiples across the portfolio: breadth, fallback depth and flags per family, with importance on a separate axis | Which families lead the licensing package |
| **Exclusivity Runway** | A timeline per asset of patent expiries, PTE, regulatory exclusivity and PTAB events, with the date-setting item highlighted | How much runway a buyer is paying for |
| **Chain of Title** | Swimlanes of UPAD conveyances: assignment, merger, license, security interest | Who can actually sign, and any distress signals |
| **Topography** | A semantic landscape with contours, client and competitor portfolios overlaid, white space marked and Solidus deals pinned onto the clusters | Where adjacent value is traded and who trades it |
| **Thicket Heatmap** | Target/indication × CPC density of mutually blocking patents | Freedom-to-operate triage and negotiating leverage |

Constellation is the view to lead with in sales and design. It is the one no incumbent can reproduce, because its size and ring encodings come from the Solidus deal ledger. The IPC/CPC overlay-map idea (a portfolio plotted on a base map of the whole technology space) is a proven, cheap companion view for Topography ([Leydesdorff et al., arXiv 1210.6456](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.6456)).

## An open data stack exists, with three licensing traps

A credible US-first stack can be built with no data licence fees, and entity resolution is where Solidus's advantage lies. PatentsView's disambiguated assignees should be resolved into the Solidus entity graph's canonical companies and ultimate parents, the same idea IPlytics markets as "Ultimate Owner" for standard-essential patents ([LexisNexis](https://www.lexisnexisip.com/resources/empowering-sep-analysts-with-insights-using-the-ultimate-owner-concept/)). Without that roll-up, Citation Lift splits one buyer across its subsidiaries.

| Google Patents Public Datasets (BigQuery) | Worldwide families and citation arrays; US full text | Query costs apply; licence terms not reviewed in this research ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/public-datasets/google-patents-public-datasets-connecting-public-paid-and-private-patent-data)) |
| EPO OPS | INPADOC families, legal status, EP Register | Free tier capped at **4 GB/week** ([epo-ops](https://github.com/patent-dev/epo-ops)); several bulk products free from 2025 per a secondary source ([patent.dev](https://patent.dev/game-changer-key-epo-patent-datasets-are-now-free/)) |
| Lens.org (incl. PatSeq, PatCite) | Scholarly cross-citations, sequences | **API commercial use requires a paid custom plan** ([Lens API terms](https://about.lens.org/lens-api-terms-of-use/)) |
| Reliance on Science (Marx & Fuegi) | Patent-to-paper links keyed to OpenAlex | **CC BY-NC**; commercial use needs the authors' permission ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/8169036)). Alternative: rebuild the links from front-page non-patent-literature citations matched to OpenAlex |
| KPSS patent values | Ground truth for a value model (public companies, to 2022) | On GitHub; public-company patents only; licence not checked ([GitHub](https://github.com/KPSS2017/Technological-Innovation-Resource-Allocation-and-Growth-Extended-Data)) |
| FDA Orange Book / Purple Book | Exclusivity Runway | Public; the Purple Book lists only dance-disclosed patents |
| Unified Patents Portal | PTAB and district-court litigation per patent | Free portal ([Unified](https://portal.unifiedpatents.com/)); redistribution terms unverified |

The traps are Lens's commercial API restriction, the non-commercial Reliance on Science licence, and the retired PatentsView API. Each has a workaround, but all three should be settled before Phase 1 code depends on them. Coverage is the structural weakness. US open data under-covers Chinese, Japanese and Korean biotech filings, and EPO OPS helps with bibliographic data but not full-text claims. That matters because Ambrosia's buyer network and conversion strength are in Asia and Europe.

## Phased build order

**Phase 1: the spine and the two metrics.** Mirror the PatentsView/ODP citation and assignee tables plus PCRD into the warehouse. Resolve assignees into the Solidus entity graph with ultimate-parent roll-up. Ship Citation Lift v1 (filer-normalized, examiner-weighted, family-to-family) and Claim Strength v1 (normalized breadth, independent count, dependency depth, raw indefinite-term counts shown beside a subjective-term tier). Ship three views: Constellation, Buyer Board and Claim Anatomy. From day one, log every ranking to the outcome ledger and run a historical backtest against Solidus licensing and M&A deals with a strict pre-deal citation window. This backtest is the go/no-go test for the product thesis.

**Phase 2: signals a buyer's counsel will ask about.** Add Office Action blocking-citation alerts, UPAD Chain of Title and the M&A radar, prosecution narrowing (application vs grant), the full indefiniteness tiering including specification-anchoring and measurement-method flags, and Exclusivity Runway with explicit derivation. Add the text-similarity weighting to Citation Lift and a portfolio-similarity term (MASS-style), then re-run the backtest and publish the change in accuracy.

**Phase 3: landscape and risk.** Build Topography (embeddings, UMAP, clusters, Solidus deal pins), Citation Flow, Thicket Heatmap and the PTAB/litigation-likeness score. Add EP families and legal status via OPS, and science linkage via OpenAlex.

**Phase 4: biotech depth and Alaric.** Add claim-type tagging (after the legal research pass on *Amgen v. Sanofi* genus and enablement risk), the sequence/CDR join to assets, AI claim charting framed as triage, and non-US coverage. Expose Citation Lift and Claim Strength as features in the shared entity graph so Solidus buyer maps and Augur investor views can use them, and so Alaric can later combine them.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of IP Map. It should not be pitched as a better patent map, because on visuals alone it competes with Derwent, PatSnap and Orbit, which have decades of data and quote-only budgets. The unoccupied ground is narrower and stronger: a buyer ranking that corrects for how much a company files, weights examiner citations over applicant ones, and is scored in public against deals that actually closed. Ambercite and PatSnap attempted this and left their methods unpublished. Solidus's deal ledger allows IP Map to publish its accuracy, and a published accuracy figure is hard for a competitor without verified deal data to answer.

Two of the brief's assumptions need adjusting before build. First, citation frequency should drive *who* to approach; deal comps should drive *what it is worth*, because the evidence splits cleanly along that line. Second, indefinite-language counts should be reframed as tiered legal-risk flags anchored to the specification, since a pharma claim reading "about 10 mg" is not the risk that "optimal" is. The largest unknown is empirical: no one has shown that citations predict pharma in-licensing partners. The Phase 1 backtest will settle that, and it should be run before significant design spend.
