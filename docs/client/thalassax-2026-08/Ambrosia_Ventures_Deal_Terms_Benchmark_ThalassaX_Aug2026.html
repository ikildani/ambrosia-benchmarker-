Three observations

1. The upfront is the outlier, not the structure. Your milestone and royalty terms sit inside the comparable range. The upfront does not. Only one transaction in the set cleared below $80M, and none of the small-molecule comparables did — Ascentage/Takeda, the closest structural analogue to Chiauranib, cleared at $100M upfront on a Phase 2 asset with ex-China rights.

2. Back-loading compounds the gap. At 1:15, your milestone-to-upfront ratio is more back-loaded than three-quarters of the comparable set, whose median is 1:10.5. A low upfront with a heavy back end is the signature of a China-origin discount already priced in — before a buyer applies one of their own.

3. Counterparty selection moves the number more than the asset does. In 2025 Hengrui licensed to GSK at $500M upfront and to Glenmark at $18M upfront. Different assets, but a 28× spread from one licensor in one year. Which counterparties are approached, in what sequence, and under what competitive tension is not a detail of execution — it is most of the outcome.

What the retained equity has to be worth

The comparison is deliberately not apples-to-apples, and the difference is the point: a NewCo trades cash today for equity upside tomorrow. So the question is not whether $35M is low — against this set it plainly is. It is whether the 25% stake covers the difference.

The bridge

To match the $200M median upfront in cash-equivalent terms, the 25% stake must be worth roughly $165M at close — implying a NewCo post-money of about $660M. Against a stated raise of $100–150M, that is the number to pressure-test before terms go to investors. At a $300M post-money — plausible for that raise — the stake is worth $75M and total consideration is roughly $110M: about half the median comparable upfront, two-thirds of it illiquid equity.

None of this argues against the NewCo route. But the trade should be sized before it is made — and these terms price the assets nearer the bottom of the comparable range than the middle.

What Phase 1 would resolve

  • The defensible upfront range for Chiauranib and CS231295, individually and as a pair — from risk-adjusted NPV, calibrated against this set rather than anchored to it.
  • NewCo versus out-licensing versus hybrid, with the equity bridge modeled properly — probability-weighted, time-discounted, tested against realistic financing scenarios.
  • Whether the pair is the right pair, and which evidence moves the upfront most per dollar spent — PDAC data of this quality is the strongest argument against a China-origin discount you have.
