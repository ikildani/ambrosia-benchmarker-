## POST 1: "The Phase 2 Inflection" (Best for engagement — contrarian, data-heavy)

---

We analyzed 3,447 biopharma licensing deals from 2020-2026.

The single most valuable moment in deal economics isn't approval. It's Phase 2 proof-of-concept.

Here's the median upfront payment by phase at signing:

Preclinical: $82M
Phase 1: $140M
Phase 2: $300M (2.1x jump)
Phase 3: $678M
Approved: $1,964M

That Phase 1 → Phase 2 jump — from $140M to $300M — is the largest single-phase multiplier in the entire development lifecycle.

For BD teams negotiating a Phase 1 out-license with near-term Phase 2 data: a 6-12 month delay could unlock >$150M in additional upfront value.

The upfront-as-percentage-of-total-deal-value also shifts:
- Preclinical: 9.7%
- Phase 2: 14.2%
- Approved: 26.5%

Translation: as clinical risk drops, licensees pay proportionally more upfront and less in milestones. The risk premium compresses.

## POST 2: "Immunology > Oncology" (Best for shares — challenges conventional wisdom)

---

Everyone assumes oncology commands the highest biopharma deal premiums.

We analyzed 3,447 deals. At Phase 2, the data says otherwise:

Phase 2 median upfront by therapeutic area:

Metabolic: $1,300M
Immunology: $1,250M
Neurology: $302M
Oncology: $281M

Immunology deals are commanding 4x the oncology upfront at Phase 2.

Why? Three forces:

1. TL1A antibodies repricing the entire autoimmune deal market
2. Validated commercial models (Dupixent, Skyrizi, Rinvoq proving $10B+ peak sales)
3. Large addressable populations + high unmet need in RA, lupus, IBD

The same dynamic is playing out in metabolic — GLP-1/obesity deals are driving Phase 2 upfronts that would have been unthinkable 3 years ago.

The conventional wisdom that "oncology = premium deals" holds in volume. Not in per-deal value.

If your pipeline spans multiple TAs, your deal timing strategy should factor in these TA-specific premiums.

## POST 3: "The ADC Normalization" (Best for thought leadership — trend narrative)

---

ADC deals had a once-in-a-generation moment in 2023.

$371.8 billion in total deal value. Median TDV of $5.9B. Pfizer-Seagen alone was $43B.

Then came the normalization:

2022: 25 deals, $3.3B median TDV
2023: 32 deals, $5.9B median TDV (peak)
2024: 35 deals, $1.8B median TDV
2025: 17 deals, $1.6B median TDV

Deal volume kept growing. But the era of $5B+ ADC platform acquisitions is over.

Here's what's actually happening:

ADCs still command a $361M median upfront — higher than bispecifics ($281M), mRNA ($156M), or gene therapy ($100M).

The modality isn't commoditized. But the market has shifted from "acquire the platform" to "license the best asset."

For ADC licensors: stop positioning on platform breadth. Focus on target differentiation, payload novelty, and Phase 2 data quality. That's where the upfront premium lives now.
