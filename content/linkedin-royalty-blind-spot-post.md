# LinkedIn Post: The Royalty Blind Spot

Data: `deals_verified`, licensing deals announced 2019-01-01 to 2026-09-28 (n = 1,083). Pulled 2026-10-14.

- Upfront disclosed: 793 (73%)
- Any royalty rate disclosed: 353 (33%)
- Median top royalty tier, where disclosed: preclinical 7.5% (n = 88), Phase 1 12% (n = 33),
  Phase 2 15% (n = 34), Phase 3 19% (n = 66), approved 19% (n = 87)

Chosen because earlier posts already covered upfront vs milestones (milestone shift, TA upfront
ratios, Phase 2 inflection). Royalties have not had a post of their own.

## POST TEXT (from Issa's personal LinkedIn profile)

On a drug that sells $1 billion a year, two points of royalty is worth $20 million a year to the company that licensed it out. Every year, for as long as the patents hold.

It's also the term companies are least likely to tell you about.

We track 1,083 licensing deals announced since 2019. Almost three quarters of them disclosed the upfront payment. Only about a third disclosed a royalty rate. The rest say "tiered royalties" and leave it there.

I understand why. The upfront is the number that goes in the headline. The royalty is the number your next partner will ask about, so nobody wants it in public.

But it leaves everyone else negotiating with very little to anchor on.

Here's what the deals that did disclose show. The median top royalty tier was about 7.5% for preclinical assets, 12% at Phase 1, 15% at Phase 2 and 19% by Phase 3.

So the royalty ceiling more than doubles between preclinical and Phase 3. And unlike the upfront, it keeps paying for the whole commercial life of the product.

In my experience the upfront gets most of the attention in a negotiation. Given the numbers, I think the royalty deserves at least as much.

For those of you who negotiate these, where do you get your royalty comps from?
