# LinkedIn Post: Telix Bought the Factory, Not the Drug

## POST TEXT (from Issa's personal LinkedIn profile)

Telix just paid $1.65B for a company whose lead drug the FDA rejected last month.

Sounds reckless.

It isn't.

Read the CRL. ITM-11 wasn't rejected for safety. Not for efficacy. For manufacturing at a third-party facility.

The clinical data held up. The plant didn't.

And the plant is what Telix really bought.

ITM makes lutetium-177, actinium-225, and terbium-161. It ships to 65+ countries. In radiopharma, that's the scarce part.

Anyone can license a targeting molecule.
Very few can make the isotope, ship it before it decays, and get it into a patient on schedule.

Now look at the structure.

$1.65B at close.
Up to $700M more, only if ITM-11 gets approved and sells.

Telix paid for the infrastructure up front.
It pays for the drug only if the drug works.

A CRL knocks down the price of an asset.
A manufacturing CRL knocks down the price of the one thing every radiopharma buyer is short on.

Telix used that discount to line up against Novartis and Pluvicto's ~$2B a year.

When you see a CRL, do you read the reason or just the headline?
